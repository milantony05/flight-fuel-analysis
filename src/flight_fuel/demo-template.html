The problem

Flight tracks show movement. Fuel use must be estimated.

Researchers studying aviation fuel need to know whether their estimates are accurate and whether the dataset includes the aircraft and flight durations they care about.

What we built

A model that estimates fuel used during completed intervals, plus an audit of prediction error and which records remain after filtering.

Who it helps

Aviation researchers and public flight-data teams checking the quality and coverage of estimated-fuel datasets.

Key finding

Quality needs two checks: error and coverage.

Full evaluation

On all 7,619 intervals, the fuel model has 96.07 kg MAE, 281.69 kg RMSE and R² of 0.872. Individual errors can still be much larger.

Selection audit

At 75% overall coverage, the group-aware rule includes 5,715 intervals with 54.02 kg MAE among those included. The group rule does not improve the underlying predictions.

Research use

Compare errors across aircraft, interval lengths and tracking availability before using estimates to study aviation fuel patterns.

Methods, limitations and evidence boundary

Fuel model training: April–August 2025; error ranker: September 1–9; main audit: September 19–27. Whole flights are separated across periods. Fuel-on-board differences are imperfect reference values; payload, weight and weather are unavailable. Zero-position intervals use a training-only aircraft-type fallback. This is a historical, offline replay, not a live fuel planner or certified emissions tool. The calibration extension is exploratory and needs an untouched later test.
